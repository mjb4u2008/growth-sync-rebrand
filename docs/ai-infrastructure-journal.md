## Editorial purpose

Explain how businesses are changing as AI becomes part of their operations. GrowthSync is building AI infrastructure for smaller businesses; Core is the first product; commerce is the first market. Becoming AI-native and staying AI-native require a maintained foundation of context, permissions, tools, execution, and evaluation.

The journal makes a specific argument, examines actual enterprise behavior, and describes practical consequences for smaller operators. It is not a news feed or a set of keyword-targeted articles. Natural's journal and Field Ventures' essays are structural references, not text to imitate.

## Eight pieces

1. A new kind of company — a smaller organization gaining more operating range.
2. The advantage underneath — JPMorgan and a wider permission to build.
3. One business, many clocks — Walmart and decisions across different operating timescales.
4. After the buildout — abundant intelligence and the remaining company-level constraint.
5. Beyond the session — durable responsibility, uncertainty, and execution.
6. The end of starting over — decisions and promises that survive changing tools.
7. The second job — reusable preparation as the test of a platform.
8. Start with the work — Tanner's source-backed inventory story and the engineering standard it sets.
